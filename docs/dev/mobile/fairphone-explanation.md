# Fairphone Explanation

## 📘 Purpose & Scope

This page explains **Fairphone** as a hardware maker and what “buying a Fairphone” usually means in practice: modularity, repair, and which operating systems you might run. It stays conceptual rather than procedural. For how **Murena**, the **e Foundation**, **/e/OS**, and **Fairphone** fit together (warranty, pricing, preinstalls), see {{ link_with_abstract("fairphone-murena-eos-explanation.md") }}.

## What is Fairphone?

**Fairphone** is a Dutch company that designs smartphones with strong emphasis on **repairability**, **spare parts**, **longer usable life**, and **more responsible sourcing** than typical consumer electronics. Their devices are sold with the idea that users (or repair shops) can replace modules—battery, screen, camera, and similar parts—instead of discarding the whole phone when one subsystem fails.

Fairphone’s approach is as much about supply chain and labor transparency as it is about screws and toolkits: the brand is positioned for buyers who want hardware aligned with environmental and social criteria, not only peak specs or lowest price.

## Software: Fairphone OS and beyond

Fairphones are commonly associated with **stock Android** distributed as **Fairphone OS**—Google-certified Android with Fairphone’s updates and policies. Buyers may also encounter devices **preloaded with /e/OS** (a privacy-oriented, deGoogled Android fork) through partners such as **Murena**, or install /e/OS and other ROMs themselves, depending on model and unlocking options.

The important distinction is threefold:

- **Hardware** (Fairphone) defines what is physically possible and supported under each sales channel’s terms.
- **Operating system** (Fairphone OS vs /e/OS vs community builds) defines privacy, default apps, and update source.
- **Seller** (Fairphone directly, a carrier, or Murena) defines bundle, price, regional availability, and often **warranty conditions** for software changes.

## Why this matters when choosing a Fairphone

If you care about **right to repair**, **openness**, or **reducing dependence on Google services**, you still choose among: buying a standard Fairphone and flashing software yourself, buying a turnkey /e/OS device from a partner, or staying on Fairphone OS. Trade-offs include support burden, cost, camera and app compatibility, and how warranty language treats bootloader unlocking or unofficial OS installs.
